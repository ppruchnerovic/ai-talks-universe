The introduction of generative AI has been a watershed moment for the Software Engineering profession. Much has been said about its effect on quality, productivity, and hiring of software engineers. In fact, so much has been said that it can be difficult to tell what is hype, what is hate, and what is real.

In this cross-disciplinary talk, co-created by a Software Engineering Manager and a Sociologist of Professions and Expertise, we present the results of a preliminary study we conducted among software engineers about the impact code-generating LLMs have on their daily work, perceived skill levels, professional training (this aspect?), and career trajectory. We also review other related research and show how these results apply to established models for the development of expertise.

Put together, this talk provides insight into the impact of LLMs on skill acquisition and expertise in software engineering and the far-reaching consequences they have on the junior-to-senior progression in software engineering. By doing so, as well as by providing context from other professions, we explore potential futures for the discipline of software engineering.

This talk is presented in collaboration with Dr. Netta Avnoon - a sociologist of professions and expertise. Netta is interested in the emergence of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a type of expertise and their integration into expert labor. Her articles have been published in Big Data and Society, New Media and Society, Work, Employment and Society, Nature Reviews Urology, the Journal of Professions and Organization and more.
