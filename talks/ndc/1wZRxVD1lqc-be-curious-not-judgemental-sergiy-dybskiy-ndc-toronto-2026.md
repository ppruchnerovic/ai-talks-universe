How curiosity — not credentials, not hype, not judgment — shaped a decade of building software.

When my family moved to Canada, I started grade 8 knowing nobody. One day my new classmates “invited” me to the movies. I showed up, bought a ticket, waited… and slowly realized no one else was coming. I didn’t know if it was a prank or a misunderstanding. Too scared to ask for a refund, I sat alone and watched Up. Something that started as discomfort became something I now love: exploring the unfamiliar on my own.

That moment became the pattern for my entire engineering career.

In this session, I’ll share a personal and technical journey across startups, Shopify, Coinbase, crypto, and eventually Sentry — and how curiosity ended up being my most underrated engineering skill. Along the way I’ll talk about misconceptions (“crypto bro”), developer culture (“real devs don’t use vibe coding tools”), and how staying curious instead of cynical led me to enter the Bolt hackathon, place second in the world, win $100,000, and pay off my parents’ mortgage.

This talk isn’t selling anything. It’s an exploration of engineering culture, personal growth, and the role curiosity plays in navigating new tools, new technologies, and new communities. Whether you love AI-assisted tooling or think it’s all slop, whether you’ve ever been misjudged, or whether you’re simply trying to stay excited about software after 10+ years — this session is food for thought.
