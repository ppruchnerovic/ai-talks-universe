## Description

Organizations are resisting the 'dark factory' model, not because of a lack of technological capability, but due to unprepared structures and mindsets. Coding agents are ready, but companies aren't set up for their seamless integration. Just like continuous delivery faced skepticism in 2009, autonomous coding agents now challenge existing frameworks and require a paradigm shift from developers as coders to orchestrators of systems. The shift isn't about better technology; it's about rethinking the roles and processes within an organization. Developers must evolve from technical craftsmanship to a conductor role in a system-driven environment, changing how teams and platforms function.

Patrick Debois, known widely as the 'godfather of DevOps', brings unique insights to this discussion. Having witnessed similar skepticism with the continuous delivery movement, Debois now observes the parallels between the challenges faced then and the resistance faced by the 'dark factory' approach today. At Tessl, where he observes first-hand the dynamics of adopting autonomous code agents, he highlights how this shift influences collaboration, team dynamics, and the role of platform teams.
