## Description

Banning interactive coding sessions might seem a step backward, but it revolutionizes software automation through the 'Context as Code' paradigm. Limiting human interference means greater efficiency in agentic development, where automation becomes more transformative by doing less manual meddling.

Dru Knox, head of product and design at Tessl, dives deep into this shift towards 'Context as Code.' With his experience leading language modeling teams at Grammarly and Cantina, Dru brings a unique insight into harness engineering and the practical applications of Tessl’s software factory. His talk balances traditional engineering methods with emerging agentic development practices.

What we cover:
• What is harness engineering and why is it challenging?
• How does Tessl's software factory utilize the triadic framework of inner, outer, and meta loops?
• Why has Tessl opted to ban interactive coding sessions?
• How do Tessl's Change Review, verifiers, and Tessl Agent manifest the software factory vision?
• How can regression testing ensure improved agent performance?
