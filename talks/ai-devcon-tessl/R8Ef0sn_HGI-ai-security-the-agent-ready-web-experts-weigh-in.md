## Description

When AI systems negotiate their own security, the ground rules of web security have to shift. Agents aren't just assisting humans. They're operating at a machine scale that humans designed systems can't easily accommodate. It sounds like science fiction, but it’s a hard truth that systems must adapt to or risk falling behind. Are you prepared for agents that can bypass traditional user controls? The data reveals a staggering 83% of enterprises plan to deploy AI, yet only 29% feel ready to do so securely, underscoring a disconnect between ambition and security readiness.

At AI Native DevCon London, Simon Maple delves into these issues with experts like Joseph Katsioloudes from GitHub, Liran Tal from Snyk, and John Groetzinger from Cisco. Maple brings together these leading voices to discuss the pressing need for new security frameworks as AI transforms from a tool to a decision-maker. With panel insights from industry leaders like Dana Lawson of Netlify, Maximiliano Firtman from codemia, and James Moss from Tessl, this conversation bridges the gap between current practices and the future of AI in web development.

What we cover:
• 83% of enterprises plan to deploy AI, but only 29% feel secure doing so
• Prompt injection is a risk that can't be fully eliminated—how can least privilege and sandboxing act as defenses?
• The concept of 'agent experience': Why systems today fail at the scale needed for machine operators
• Do fundamentals like HTTP, semantic HTML, and accessibility still matter when agents do the bulk of work?
• Why blocking WebMCP when agents are involved is like trying to turn off the sun
