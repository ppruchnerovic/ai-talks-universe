## Description

In Tessl's 'Dark Factory,' 95% of the code ships without a human ever looking at it. This isn't just automation—it's reimagining how we think about code review and quality assurance. It might sound risky, yet the team not only maintains output but accelerates it, clearing hundreds of pull requests a week, even during weekends. It's not about replacing developers. It's about expanding what they can achieve without barriers.

Rob Willoughby, AI engineering lead at Tessl, dives deep into the mechanics behind this autonomous code powerhouse. His team navigates between orchestrators, natural language 'verifiers,' and some surprising failures that required reinventing trust from scratch. With his guidance, learn how Tessl runs 65-70% of its PRs through machines and what stakes this process involves.

What we cover:
• How Tessl routes 65-70% of its pull requests through an autonomous 'Dark Factory'
• Why does context in the repository matter more than your model choice?
• The role of natural language 'verifiers' in transforming code review
