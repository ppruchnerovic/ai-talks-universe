## Description

A software factory can ship code autonomously, but security, ops, finance, sales and compliance still each want to approve what goes out. Baruch Sadogursky, Head of DevRel at Port, borrows characters from The Phoenix Project to show why none of those approvals are really about code. He maps what each stakeholder needs, where that evidence actually lives, and how a context lake plus governed agents, MCPs and skills turns approval into signals, building a factory of factories. Recorded at AI DevCon New York 2026.

What we cover:
– Why every stakeholder's 'as long as' can stall a software factory
– What approval really means for engineering, security, ops, finance, sales and compliance
– Why most of the evidence lives outside the repository
– Pouring context from many tools into a context lake
– Onboarding agents, MCPs and skills with ownership, permissions and spend controls
– Why regulators care about how you control what you do
