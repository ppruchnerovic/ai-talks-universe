## Description

Most engineering teams shipping AI agents have no idea whether their agents are actually following instructions. You add a skill, deploy a prompt, watch the output, and call it done. But output evals only tell you what came out. They don't tell you whether the agent used the right tools, followed the right steps, or quietly ignored half of what you told it to do. This session introduces the distinction between output evals and trajectory evals, and shows you real trace data on agent behavior.

Simon Obstbaum and Rob Willoughby deliver insights into this complex evaluative landscape. With expertise grounded in Tessl's eval infrastructure and cutting-edge research from Stanford University's cross-company measurement project, they offer a framework applicable across diverse AI platforms like Claude Code, Cursor, and Gemini.

What we cover:
• The difference between output evals and trajectory evals
• Instrumenting agent behavior with real trace data
• Why separating activation, trajectory, and outcome is essential
• How to define meaningful coverage metrics
• What to instrument for better agent performance
