## Description

Developers often hit a wall when managing more than four AI agents simultaneously. Despite shipping twice as many pull requests with AI, merge rates have dropped from 80% to 60%, raising questions about AI efficiency. The limitation lies in human attention: managing multiple agents leads to oversight and forgotten tasks. This bottleneck is not about the tools' capability but the cognitive load on developers. It's a paradoxical situation where tools like Claude Code promise efficiency, yet complexity stalls progress. Nick Arcolano, Head of AI & Research at Jellyfish, brings an immense dataset to the discussion. With data from 250,000 developers and over 40 million data points, Nick provides insights into the intricacies of agentic coding. Through monthly benchmarks across enterprises, he reveals why experienced engineers hit barriers and what it takes to overcome them.

What we cover:
• Why do engineers hit a wall at four concurrent agents?
• Understanding the 80% vs 60% merge rate gap between AI and human PRs
• 71% weekly active AI usage and the importance of 'depth of use'
• How should engineering leads prepare for 2026 as CFOs demand evidence?
• What misconceptions do engineering leads have about achieving AI-native development?
• Why do companies with advanced tools still build incrementally, and what changes in architecture are needed?
