## Description

For a given internal pain point, Brian Lewis might find 10 to 15 startups worth a look, book two or three demos, run zero or one pilot, and sign roughly one contract for every four pilots. That works out to about 5% of demo calls ending in a signature, which turns out to match published benchmarks. He is on the buying side at a hedge fund, speaking personally rather than for his employer, and the talk is a catalogue of exactly where the other 95% dies.

The examples are specific enough to sting. A vendor asked them to self report their own gateway telemetry so it could charge margin on traffic that never touched vendor infrastructure. Integrations that only function if granted read and write access to everything. Features held permanently in beta because the beta terms permit data retention. A team claiming zero data retention that revealed it had their data by mentioning something it could not otherwise have seen. Asked what happens in a breach, one vendor answered that they had not had one yet. His larger argument is that none of this is really an AI problem: a new frontier model lands every 11 days while the architecture underneath is a decade old, and he puts perhaps 60% of becoming AI native on entitlements, integration and change management. Agents inherit whatever foundations you already had.
