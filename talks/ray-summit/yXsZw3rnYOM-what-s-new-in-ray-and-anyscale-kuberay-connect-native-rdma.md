## Description

Anyscale and Nscale are coming together to build a vertically integrated, AI-native computing stack, optimized across every layer from power to tokens. That news opens the Day 1 keynote, followed by every Ray and Anyscale announcement of the summit.

Anyscale CEO Keerti Melkote makes the case for owning your intelligence through continual learning loops, then sits down with Josh Payne, founder and CEO of Nscale, to talk through what the combination means for Ray, for Anyscale's multi-cloud strategy, and for the developers building on both, including Nscale's commitment of over 300,000 GPUs over the next two years and Ray remaining open source and community-governed under the PyTorch Foundation.

Robert Nishihara, creator of Ray, follows with the anatomy of a reinforcement learning workload and why it is one of the hardest distributed systems problems in AI. The Ray team then covers a year of development: cgroup-based resource isolation on Kubernetes, the new Ray history server, 10x faster startup for 32,000 actors, topology-aware scheduling for multi-host TPUs and NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs, Ray Direct Transport in beta, Ray Data scaling to 5,000 GPUs, Ray Sandboxes launching 100,000 sandboxes in 30 seconds, and Ray Serve LLM reaching general availability. Christian Stano, Field CTO, maps the platform stack from one cluster to many clouds, followed by a live demo of coding agents building a complete learning loop and two announcements: Anyscale KubeRay Connect, which connects an existing KubeRay cluster to Anyscale in minutes with no migration, and Anyscale GPU health observability, which takes hardware root cause analysis from hours to seconds.
