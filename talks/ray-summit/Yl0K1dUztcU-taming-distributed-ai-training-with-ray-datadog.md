## Description

At Ray Summit 2025, Savita Manghnani from Datadog shares how Datadog keeps large-scale Ray training workloads fast, reliable, and observable—turning opaque, multi-GPU distributed jobs into systems engineers can monitor, debug, and optimize with confidence.

She begins by outlining the messy realities of training LLMs and AI models at scale: thousands of tasks running across heterogeneous GPU clusters, jobs stalling without clear causes, GPUs going idle unexpectedly, and slowdowns that are difficult to attribute. While Ray provides the distributed execution foundation, effective observability is what makes large-scale workloads predictable and operable.

Savita then dives into the real-world challenges Datadog encounters when running Ray internally to power LLM training, along with the observability techniques the team developed to address them. She covers:

Common failure modes in large Ray deployments—task backpressure, resource fragmentation, object store contention, spillover, slow nodes, and scheduler bottlenecks

Which signals matter most, including the specific metrics, traces, and logs that actually help diagnose bottlenecks and failures

How correlating these signals transforms Ray training from a “black box” into a transparent, debuggable system

Techniques Datadog uses to identify GPU underutilization, track straggler tasks, analyze scheduling delays, and detect systemic issues before they escalate

Finally, Savita shows how these observability practices make distributed AI workloads more reliable, explainable, and performant, whether you're training your first multi-node model or operating production-scale LLM jobs.

Attendees will walk away with actionable strategies for building observability into their Ray clusters—and for turning large distributed training jobs into systems they can truly understand and improve.
