## Description

In this bi-weekly meeting of the OWASP Generative AI Security Project, core contributors gathered for project updates, community news, and working group check-ins—alongside some light banter about travel, weather, and the “agent party” energy building around upcoming events in Europe.

A major highlight was a milestone announcement: the project’s LinkedIn community surpassed 20,000 followers, reflecting rapid growth in global interest and participation. Scott previewed additional “state of the project” metrics soon to be published, including the scale of peer-reviewed outputs and the growing footprint of derivative works—where project resources are being reused and adapted across the wider security ecosystem. The team is also building agent-driven tracking to better quantify adoption and references across industry.

Key project updates included:

Agentic AI Security Summit (London / Black Hat Europe): A reminder that the Agentic AI Security Summit is approaching quickly, co-located with Black Hat Europe. The summit will serve as a major community gathering and the launch venue for the Agentic Top 10.

Solutions Landscape (Q3/Q4 update): Scott shared updates to the Solutions Landscape resources, including the quarterly matrix and supporting cheat sheets that map tools across lifecycle stages (LLM SecOps and Agentic SecOps). The project now tracks 150+ solutions, reflecting how fast the ecosystem is expanding. The next release will add a dedicated red teaming lens and restructure the content to be more digestible (taxonomy page + targeted breakouts).

Top 10 for LLM Applications — upcoming update: The group discussed planning for the next refresh of the core LLM Top 10, noting that many emerging issues have now shifted into the agentic domain. Key open questions include whether the update should be a minor revision or major overhaul, whether rankings should be adjusted, and how best to incorporate stronger evidence and data collection. A particularly lively thread—revisited again—is whether prompt injection should be treated as a “vulnerability” vs. a broader class of failure mode and control breakdown.

CTI Initiative: The Cyber Threat Intelligence team announced a move to monthly meetings (first Friday schedule) to boost engagement and improve delivery alignment. Upcoming work includes a deepfake guide update (targeted for December) and continued exploit research, with results now realistically targeted for end of Q1 2026 due to volunteer availability and holiday timing.

Red Teaming Initiative: Jason reported strong progress and renewed momentum under a co-lead structure. The handbook is structured across phases and emphasizes “real world” AI red teaming—beyond raw model jailbreaks—focusing on testing deployed systems, agent behaviors, and practical execution. A call was made for more practitioners with hands-on experience to contribute targeted exploitation scenarios. The team also addressed onboarding friction due to recent Slack invite-link changes and pointed contributors to the official contribution pathways.

The meeting closed with a discussion about coordinating cadence across many active subgroups (agentic, red teaming, CTI, etc.) to avoid burnout while maintaining meaningful updates and cross-initiative alignment.

To follow the project and access resources, visit the OWASP GenAI Security Project website: https://genai.owasp.org
