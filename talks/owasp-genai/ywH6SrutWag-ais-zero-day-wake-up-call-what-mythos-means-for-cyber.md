# AI’s Zero-Day Wake-Up Call: What Mythos Means for Cyber Defense

## Description

In this episode of The Threat Intelligence Podcast, Rachel James and Brian Nakayama of the OWASP GenAI AI Threat Intelligence Initiative about Anthropic’s Mythos model and the changing role of AI in vulnerability discovery.

Rachel explains how earlier AI models offered limited value to attackers unless the user already had significant technical expertise. Those systems could help with pieces of a task, but they struggled to reason through vulnerabilities or generate reliable exploit paths on their own. Mythos, by contrast, appears to demonstrate a more advanced ability to analyze source code, understand how functions behave, identify weaknesses, and assist in building working exploits.

Brian brings a measured view to the discussion, emphasizing that the real-world impact is still developing. Mythos seems strongest in white-box testing, where the model has access to code, but there is still limited evidence about how effective similar systems will be in black-box attacks or full attack orchestration. The conversation also explores cost, exploit yield, AI harnesses, open-source model competition, and the possibility that advanced capabilities could spread through distillation or cheaper tooling.

The episode also looks at Project Glasswing, a controlled-access program designed to give vendors time to find and fix vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them. While this is a positive sign that AI companies are trying to act responsibly, it also creates a new risk: access to powerful AI security tools may become a target for attackers, especially through stolen credentials and API keys.

The central message is that defenders have been given a head start, but not much of one. AI is unlikely to make every low-skill attacker instantly dangerous, but it may help well-resourced actors move faster. Organizations should prepare now by tightening API key security, reassessing patch timelines, strengthening vulnerability management, reviewing bug bounty and disclosure processes, and making sure they have the people and procedures needed to respond when exploitation windows shrink.
