## Description

In this episode of The Threat Intelligence Podcast, Brian Nakayama and Rachel James of the OWASP GenAI Security Project about one of the most common and misunderstood threats facing AI systems: prompt injection.

The conversation begins with the unusual story of Grok, Bankerbot, and DRB tokens, where an attacker reportedly used Morse code to bypass safeguards and trigger an automated cryptocurrency transaction. Brian explains how this incident illustrates a core weakness of large language models: they are designed to follow instructions, even when those instructions are disguised, encoded, or hidden in ways traditional filters may miss.

Rachel connects the incident to the OWASP GenAI concept of excessive agency. The real danger is not just that an AI model can be tricked, but that organizations are giving AI systems the ability to take meaningful actions without enough human oversight. When an AI can transfer money, issue refunds, make purchases, change records, or act on untrusted internet content, prompt injection becomes more than a technical flaw—it becomes a business risk.

The episode also explores indirect prompt injection, where malicious instructions are hidden on websites, in invisible text, or in other online content that AI systems may retrieve and trust. As more businesses rush to deploy chatbots, agents, and automated workflows, Brian and Rachel warn that organizations should assume these systems will be attacked.

The key takeaway is simple: just because AI can be added to a workflow does not mean it should be. Businesses should use layered defenses, limit what AI systems can access, narrow trusted data sources, require human review for high-risk actions, and ask vendors direct questions about prompt injection protections. For small businesses, startups, and companies preparing for investment or acquisition, AI security planning should be part of the business plan from day one.
