## Description

Andrew D'Souza, CEO/Founder, Boardy

About the Speaker:
Andrew D’Souza is the Founder and CEO of Boardy, the AI superconnector that has become every founder’s first call when they need to close a round, make a key hire, or land a customer. Boardy learns a founder’s story and connects them to the right investors, talent, and partners through high-trust introductions. Andrew previously co-founded Clearco, deploying $2.5B+ into 10,000+ startups as one of the world’s largest e-commerce investors.

Graham is a recognized expert in databases, artificial intelligence, distributed systems, and computational linguistics, specializing in developing complex distributed systems and Cloud/IoT technologies at the scale of hundreds of millions of users, thousands of nodes.
As a leader, he has held a range of critical roles over the last 30 years, most recently helping to lead Wattpad to a successful exit.
As a technologist, Graham has led teams to many patents and papers, all the while transitioning breakthrough research into innovative products, and holds a number of patents himself in these areas.
He has also been a leadership coach for over a decade, and maintains an unshakeable belief in people - in particular, growing and nurturing high performing, inclusive teams and leaders.
Graham holds a BMath in Computer Science and Software Engineering from the University of Waterloo.

Abstract:
Networking and relationship capital are among the most consequential — and least systematized — inputs to business outcomes. At Boardy, we rebuilt the entire logic of professional introductions using AI: from how we evaluate supply quality, to how we prioritize matches, to how we make real-time decisions about who gets connected to whom and why. This talk walks through the strategic and executive decisions that shaped Boardy's AI architecture — not the engineering, but the why behind the build. Andrew will cover how Boardy's leadership team approached the classic build vs. buy tension, how they defined ROI for AI investments without clean historical data, and what frameworks emerged from operating an AI-native company at the intersection of trust, relationships, and automation.
