Sponsored Session: Code, Control, and Community: How Open Source is Reshaping Innovation Strategy - David Hirsch, Dynatrace

This session focuses on the strategic dilemma faced by software companies in choosing between Open Source and Internal Development to drive innovation and technological disruption. The central research question explores how each approach influences innovation outcomes, competitive advantage, and market adoption—highlighting the trade-offs between community-driven collaboration and proprietary control.

Using a comparative case study methodology, the research analyzes real-world examples from the Observability, Cloud Native, and Artificial Intelligence domains. Companies and projects are examined to assess the practical effects of each strategy on key dimensions: speed of innovation, scalability, operational efficiency, security, trust, cost, and customer satisfaction.

Do Open Source strategies accelerate innovation, expand adoption, and reduce development costs through community contributions?

Does Internal Development offer stronger control over intellectual property, quality, and security—particularly critical in regulated or enterprise environments?

This session discusses a decision-making framework to guide organizations in selecting the most effective strategy—or combination—based on their specific context. The framework incorporates strategic, technical, and organizational criteria to help leaders evaluate trade-offs, align innovation choices with business goals, and avoid common pitfalls such as strategic lock-in or underutilized community potential.

In an era where openness increasingly defines competitive advantage, this session aims to empower both established enterprises and startups to make informed, context-aware choices that support long-term sustainability, innovation, and customer value.
