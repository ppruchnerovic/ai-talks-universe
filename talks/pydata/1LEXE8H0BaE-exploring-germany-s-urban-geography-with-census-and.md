Description:
Urban geography examines the distribution of resources and infrastructure to identify systemic disadvantages in city layouts. A primary challenge in this field is the fragmentation of spatial data, which often requires consolidating disparate sources to perform meaningful accessibility analysis.

To address this, a research workflow was developed using a PostgreSQL database with PostGIS for spatial queries. Census 2022 data, available at 100-meter resolutions, was imported via a custom Python tool, census2pgsql. OpenStreetMap data was integrated using pgosmflex, while a verified dataset from the German statistics office provided precise hospital locations and care levels. Network distances and travel costs were calculated using the Open Route Service, moving beyond simple straight-line measurements to realistic road-network travel times.

Analysis of hospital accessibility by car revealed that 98.4% of the population lives within 30 minutes of a facility. However, 15-minute access showed greater variance across age groups, with the 18-29 cohort showing higher accessibility, likely due to higher concentrations in urban centers near university clinics. Regional disparities were also evident, with eastern German states showing lower access to high-level care facilities. The final results were visualized using MapLibre and Tippecanoe to generate interactive vector tiles.
