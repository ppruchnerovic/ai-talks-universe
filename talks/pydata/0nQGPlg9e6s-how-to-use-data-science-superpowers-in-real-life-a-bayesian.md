Description:
In this presentation, Tim Lenzen explores how data science methodologies, particularly Bayesian inference, can be applied to real-life decision-making scenarios. Drawing from his experience as a Senior Data Scientist, Lenzen contrasts workplace data science problems with personal decisions, examining the key differences in data availability, resources, and success metrics. Through a practical example of preparing for a cloud certification exam, he demonstrates how Bayesian modeling using PyMC can transform intuition and limited data into quantifiable decision support. The presentation illustrates the workflow of defining prior distributions, incorporating new data, and deriving actionable insights from posterior distributions. Lenzen emphasizes that while such detailed analysis may not be practical for every decision, the Bayesian framework offers valuable principles for structured decision-making and improved communication about assumptions and evidence. He addresses the challenges of applying these methods to complex life choices, such as career changes or relocations, where metrics are multifaceted and data is scarce. The talk concludes by advocating for the thoughtful application of data science principles to enhance both personal and societal decision-making processes, while acknowledging the importance of managing uncertainty.
