## Description

Most teams evaluate AI accuracy by running a handful of test queries, picking whichever system looks best, and shipping. The problem is that ad-hoc testing doesn't reflect production behavior and isn't replicable; worse, standard benchmarks aren't calibrated to specific use cases. When you're making infrastructure decisions that cost hundreds of thousands of dollars, cherry-picked demos are not a measurement strategy. This talk introduces a rigorous framework for evaluating AI search quality that applies classical ML statistical principles to a problem the industry has largely treated as subjective. I’ll cover how to construct a golden test set that actually predicts production performance, how to validate LLMs-as-judge against human raters before trusting them at scale, and how to run controlled comparisons across providers. The deeper argument is about what most evaluations get wrong at the measurement level. LLM-based evaluation is inherently stochastic, so the same query, run through the same system, produces different outputs across trials. Reporting accuracy alone can't tell you whether a performance difference is real or just noise; I’ll explain how to decompose evaluation variance into signal and instability. You’ll leave with a methodology you can apply immediately to your own vendor comparisons and internal eval pipelines.
