## Description

As AI agents move from demos into production, many teams discover that a more powerful model does not automatically produce better results. The real challenge is often context: giving the agent the right information, at the right time, in the right form. This session shares lessons learned from building and scaling Incident Manager Assist, an enterprise multi-agent system designed to support complex incident investigations. As adoption grew and more enterprise data was connected, we encountered recurring problems: too much information overwhelmed the model, long-running investigations lost important decisions, answers were not tailored to the user, operational data became stale, and expensive models were used even for simple tasks. These challenges led us to six practical context engineering patterns: model-driven control flow, scoped context assembly, intent-driven layered memory, user-aware context, task-adaptive model routing, and continuous context. Attendees will leave with a clear way to think about context, and practical patterns for making context-aware agents reliable in production.
