## Description

Dr. Samantha T. Arundel, Senior Science Advisor, National Geospatial Program, U.S. Geological Survey

An Intelligent National Map: Coordinating Structured Analysis and Scientific Validation Across Authoritative Geospatial Data

The U.S. Geological Survey’s National Map provides a consistent, authoritative foundation for elevation, hydrography, transportation, and land cover data across the United States. However, the increasing complexity of spatial problems has outpaced platforms built around static layer access or manual analysis. An Intelligent National Map (INM) is being developed as a modular framework that supports structured queries, coordinated model workflows, and transparent review of analytic results. Rather than serving as a single tool, an INM organizes existing federal datasets into reusable workflows that support spatial reasoning and policy-relevant analysis.
Each workflow is initiated through a user query and proceeds through configured steps for data retrieval, transformation, modeling, and review. All inputs are drawn directly from agency-maintained sources, including the 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), the National Hydrography Dataset (NHD), and land cover datasets such as LCMAP. Results are tied to specific records and returned with complete provenance documentation. Instead of replicating or warehousing data, an INM issues structured queries against live repositories using defined interfaces and schema constraints.
Workflows are carried out by task-specific agents, each aligned to a discrete operation such as model selection, input filtering, or threshold evaluation. Validation is handled by agents configured to compare analytic outputs to published values, ScienceBase records, or ontologically defined spatial relationships. For example, a workflow to delineate flood exposure may combine current stream gauge data with elevation derivatives and surface connectivity metrics to identify risk zones, which are then checked for agreement with hazard maps or published thresholds.
The platform supports use cases in hazard response, infrastructure planning, and environmental monitoring. Examples include wetland persistence mapping based on hydrologic connectivity, optimization of transportation alignments using topographic cost surfaces, and comparison of projected land cover scenarios for zoning evaluation. Results are returned as standardized spatial products along with intermediate diagnostics that document key parameter choices and processing steps.
Every analysis includes a structured explanation layer. Users can view variable contributions, input sources, and references checked by validation agents. Review modules simulate internal steps used in USGS publication workflows, including peer review and policy compliance checks. These are configured using synthetic review personas aligned to the USGS Fundamental Science Practices framework.
Initial development is focused on elevation and hydrography workflows where classification methods and source data are stable. Ongoing work includes refining query interpretation, extending validation loops, and establishing audit mechanisms consistent with governance guidance from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. Long-term planning includes cross-domain integration and federation across federal organizations.
