# Chetan Conikee: Your agents' traces are your most valuable asset: reading the software factory floor

## Description

A modern harness does not click buttons. It writes a shell line, a pipe, an inline script, and hands it to an interpreter. It does that in step with one inference backend, the frontier model it was trained against and benchmarked with. Swap the harness and you have not measured efficiency; you have changed the model, the prompt cache, the tool vocabulary and the compaction rules at once. Olki sits beside the harness you already use—Codex and OpenCode—and records what actually ran. Each line becomes typed events, one per stage, with the program, its arguments, and what it touched. From that record, four readers get their own view: the organization across machines, the inference provider counting repeated failures and the cache that carries the bill, and the product owner asking how agents use the tool they shipped. This session walks the software factory floor: how your harness interacts with the inference provider stack, how a prefix cache lives, why turn two must reach turn one's node, what a subagent or a model switch costs, and what you can only learn by reading the record instead of swapping the harness.
