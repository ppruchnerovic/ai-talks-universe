## Description

Training LLMs for entertainment applications, such as AI characters and interactive storytelling, presents a unique opportunity: how to develop models that are engaging and safe. Measuring model responses is a specialization where there is not merely a set universe of objective criteria, as in other fields. As a result, we often use signals from user behavior, such as swipe interactions and whether a user continues the conversation or disengages, to infer preferences and guide model improvements. This talk will explore how we approach post-training in this setting using reinforcement learning with real-world user interaction data. We will discuss how to identify which user behaviors are meaningful and how to use them to improve models despite the lack of a clear ground truth. We will share practical techniques for balancing multiple goals. This includes methods for structuring training so the model learns from both positive signals (e.g., continued engagement) and negative signals (e.g., undesirable outputs), as well as strategies for prioritizing different objectives at different stages of training. We will also cover how testing and training models on difficult prompts helps uncover weaknesses and improve reliability in real-world use, and how improvements discovered in larger models can be transferred to smaller, more efficient models without sacrificing performance. Grounded in real-world implementation, this session will provide a clear framework for training and improving LLMs in domains where success is difficult to measure directly. Attendees will leave with practical approaches to working with imperfect user feedback, balancing competing objectives, and applying these methods to production AI systems.
