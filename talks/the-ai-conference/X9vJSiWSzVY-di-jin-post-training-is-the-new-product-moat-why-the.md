## Description

Two years ago, post-training was a research term and a checkbox on an inference bill. Today Cursor, Cognition, Harvey and others ship their own models, built on open weights and sharpened with reinforcement learning — not because they wanted to become model labs, but because prompting a general model was no longer good enough for their products. This talk traces how that shift happened and what it means for anyone building AI products. I'll cover the four preconditions that made it possible: open-weight models good enough to specialize, verifiable rewards that made RL practical outside a lab, agents that raised the bar from plausible answers to reliable task completion, and the economics of a small custom model beating a frontier API. I'll then look at how the vendor landscape has organized itself — who sells post-training today, and how much of the work each option leaves in your hands. Drawing on post-training Llama 3 and 4 at Meta, co-founding Eigen AI, and now leading AI training at Nebius, I'll share how we approach this at Nebius: an AI cloud where training tooling — both our own and our partners' — sits directly on the compute, so teams can post-train their own models without stitching together a stack. I'll ground this in a recent customer case and close with three predictions about where post-training goes next.
