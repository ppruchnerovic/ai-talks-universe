## Description

We've moved from AI that generates answers to AI that takes actions — and it's broken most of our existing product design playbooks. This talk shares concrete lessons from building agentic AI products at Google Labs, including Project Mariner (an autonomous web agent), Opal (chained AI workflows), and Pomelli (AI marketing for SMBs), alongside a personal experiment: orchestrating a team of 14 AI agents for everything from meal planning to publishing a coloring book on Amazon KDP. The hard-earned lesson? Agentic products live in a year of tensions — exciting vs. overwhelming, autonomy vs. control, thinking big while shipping fast — and the teams that ship well are the ones who design for these tensions instead of pretending they don't exist. What attendees will take away: • A risk framework for agent autonomy based on reversibility, blast radius, and risk type (reputational, monetary, legal) — because "when should the agent ask permission?" is the central design question and the answer is never one-size-fits-all • The "Do It With Me" vs. "Do It For Me" spectrum — a practical model for calibrating how much user involvement any given task should have, with real examples from shipping • Why designing for imperfection matters more than designing for accuracy — agents are non-deterministic systems, and the winning design principle is humans-in-the-loop as a feature, not a fallback • Iterative co-discovery: how users and agents think together, and why scope expansion through collaboration is not scope creep — it is the product working as intended • A proactive design quadrant for finding magic (not Clippy) — when proactive agents feel delightful vs. intrusive, based on task value and user effort This talk is grounded in real product work, real failures, and the honest gap between "AI agent demo" and "AI agent daily driver." No product pitches — just frameworks and lessons from building at both Google scale and personal scale.
