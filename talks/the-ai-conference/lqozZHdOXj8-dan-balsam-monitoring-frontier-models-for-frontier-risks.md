## Description

An OpenAI model breaking containment and attacking Hugging Face this summer proved that far-off frontier AI risks are now everyday AI risks. The industry is months away from an open model capable of the same damage, or worse, in higher-stakes emerging fields like biology. We need to prepare for these emerging risks by building guardrails that catch reward hacking, offensive cyberattacks, and biorisk in production, because these and other risks still slip past today's standard monitoring approaches. In this talk, Dan Balsam will present an approach to frontier risk guardrails that "reads the mind of the model," using a model's internal representations to catch reward hacking and other harmful behavior as it happens. He'll share case studies from production guardrails across risk categories like cyber and biology, discuss benefits for cost and performance, and what defenders can build into their models now to protect against frontier risks. Bio: Dan Balsam is co-founder and CTO of Goodfire, an AI research lab using interpretability to understand, learn from and design AI systems.
