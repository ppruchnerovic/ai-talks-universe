## Description

Training gets the headlines, but inference pays the bills, and for most AI products, it's where margins are won or lost. Today, the vast majority of inference runs on big cloud providers or abstraction platforms, where convenience is high but cost visibility is low. Teams that carefully optimize their models often ship them on infrastructure chosen by default, then discover at scale that their fastest-growing cost is also their least understood. This session is a hands-on framework for understanding inference unit economics in practice. We'll start with the problem every team eventually faces: usage grows, the invoice grows faster, and nobody can quite explain the relationship between the two, because layers of managed abstraction hide the variables that drive cost. The core of the talk is a step-by-step method for making those variables visible. We'll break down how to calculate your true cost per request (not just the sticker price), why utilization — not hardware price — is the number that matters most, and how workload shape (batch vs. real-time, bursty vs. steady, token-heavy vs. compute-light) should drive infrastructure choices. And we'll challenge a common assumption along the way: GPUs aren't the only answer. A growing share of production inference runs efficiently on CPU-only servers, and knowing when that trade-off wins is part of the framework. With the model in hand, we'll map the decision landscape honestly: serverless APIs, managed GPU endpoints, dedicated GPU compute, and CPU-based inference each win in different scenarios, and attendees will get a simple break-even approach for knowing when to move between them. We'll close with a brief look at how these principles play out on dedicated infrastructure: one example of how teams that own their inference stack turn cost from a mystery into a lever. Attendees will walk away with a clear AI infra framework, a checklist for auditing their current inference spend, and a clear-eyed way to answer the question every engineering lead eventually gets asked: "why is our AI bill this high, and what are we doing about it?"
